Talking Walls

This interactive audio visual artwork explores the feeling of pressure inside a closed room. The digital 3D room is the main visual element. Traditionally, a room functions as a symbol of safety, privacy, and protection. In this piece, that symbol is questioned and transformed.

When the viewer enters the webpage, the room appears calm and stable. Only after clicking does the sound begin. The soundscape consists of muffled voices, loud talking, and the distant sound of a child crying. Everything feels as if it is happening behind a wall. The words are not clearly understandable, but their presence is clearly noticeable.

From a semiotic perspective, the room acts as a signifier of stability and shelter. The voices function as another sign that represents social or emotional pressure. The interpretant is a feeling of tension and claustrophobia.

As the volume increases, the walls move inward from the sides. This reaction is not realistic architecture but a symbolic translation of sound into space. The louder the voices become, the smaller the room feels. In this way, the sound becomes an index of pressure because it directly causes the visual change.

Without the sound, the room appears neutral and safe. Without the visual space, the voices remain abstract. Only together do they form a complete semiotic system.
